Describe what we look at, never the checklist itself. checks: - title: Secrets in the app bundle text: >- Payment, AI, database, and email keys compiled into the app, where anyone can pull them out, and paid APIs the app calls directly instead of through your server. - title: Tokens and data on the device text: >- Sign-in tokens in plain storage instead of the Keychain or Keystore, local databases left unencrypted, and data and push tokens that stay behind after logout. - title: Deep links, WebViews, and redirects text: >- Links that open screens or take actions without checks, sign-in redirects another app can intercept, and WebViews that load untrusted pages with a bridge into the app. - title: Network and platform settings text: >- Cleartext traffic and security exceptions, exported Android components, backups and debug flags, permissions nothing uses, and logging left in release builds. - title: Speed and stability text: >- Long lists that render everything, work that blocks the UI, oversized images and slow startup, and crash reporting that can't tell you where a crash happened. - title: Store and version requirements text: >- Target SDK and Xcode requirements, privacy manifests and data labels, in-app account deletion, signing keys and store account ownership, and a way to force an update. why: |- Every audit runs scanners like gitleaks, osv-scanner, and Semgrep against your code, but few scanners understand mobile app configuration, and the best ones need a built app. The findings that hurt most in mobile apps are usually ones a scanner can't see: - A server key in an environment variable that the build inlines into the app - A deep link that opens a payment screen with whatever the link says - A login token left in plain storage after the user signs out - An app with no minimum version, so a broken release stays on phones for months Those come from reading the code, and from building and shipping mobile apps of our own. upgrades: |- React Native maintains only its latest three minor releases, Expo ships about three SDKs a year, and Flutter fixes only its current stable release. Every mobile audit checks your framework and SDK versions against the official support policies, and your native libraries against the versions you're on. The stores force upgrades too: Google Play now requires new apps and updates to target Android 16 (API level 36), and Apple requires builds from Xcode 26. If you're behind, the report lays out the upgrade step by step, and Standard and Comprehensive also list the libraries that stand in the way.
